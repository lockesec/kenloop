# References

Published work that informs the design choices. Credits for code and adapted ideas are in the [README](../README.md).

- FSRS: J. Ye, J. Su, Y. Cao, "A Stochastic Shortest Path Algorithm for
  Optimizing Spaced Repetition Scheduling", KDD 2022.
- Spacing before a dated exam: N. J. Cepeda, E. Vul, D. Rohrer, J. T. Wixted,
  H. Pashler, "Spacing effects in learning: a temporal ridgeline of optimal
  retention", *Psychological Science*, 2008.
- Confident errors reviewed first: B. Butterfield, J. Metcalfe, "Errors
  committed with high confidence are hypercorrected", *JEP: LMC*, 2001.
- Retention across separate days: K. A. Rawson, J. Dunlosky, L. M.
  Sciartelli, "The power of successive relearning", *Educational Psychology
  Review*, 2013.
- Mixed sessions: D. Rohrer, "Interleaving helps students distinguish among
  similar concepts", *Educational Psychology Review*, 2012.
- Pretesting in the tutor rules: L. E. Richland, N. Kornell, L. S. Kao, "The
  pretesting effect", *JEP: Applied*, 2009.
- Strict credit and the hint ladder: E. L. Bjork, R. A. Bjork, "Desirable
  difficulties in theory and practice", *JARMAC*, 2020.
- Practice testing and spacing as high-utility techniques: J. Dunlosky et al.,
  "Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques", *PSPI*,
  2013.
- The learner does the reasoning (the graded dialogue, the `question` move):
  K. Bisra, Q. Liu, J. C. Nesbit, F. Salimi, P. H. Winne, "Inducing
  self-explanation: A meta-analysis", *Educational Psychology Review*, 2018
  (prompted self-explanation, g = .55, and ahead of explanations given by the
  instructor); M. T. H. Chi, N. de Leeuw, M.-H. Chiu, C. LaVancher, "Eliciting
  self-explanations improves understanding", *Cognitive Science*, 1994;
  B. Rosenshine, C. Meister, S. Chapman, "Teaching students to generate
  questions: A review of the intervention studies", *Review of Educational
  Research*, 1996.
- Curiosity and memory: G. Loewenstein, "The psychology of curiosity: A review
  and reinterpretation", *Psychological Bulletin*, 1994 (curiosity as a felt
  gap in knowledge); M. J. Kang et al., "The wick in the candle of learning:
  Epistemic curiosity activates reward circuitry and enhances memory",
  *Psychological Science*, 2009; M. J. Gruber, B. D. Gelman, C. Ranganath,
  "States of curiosity modulate hippocampus-dependent learning via the
  dopaminergic circuit", *Neuron*, 2014. These are small lab studies with
  trivia questions. Grading curiosity by what the learner does (asks, chooses
  to look) rather than by self-report is a design choice, not a tested finding.
